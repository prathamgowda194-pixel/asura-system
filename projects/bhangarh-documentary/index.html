ACT II // 1573 AD : THE GOLDEN FOUNDATION

The Imperial Vision of Raja Madho Singh

In 1573 AD, Raja Bhagwant Das, the ruler of Amber, established Bhangarh for his second son, Madho Singh I. Madho Singh’s elder brother was the fabled General Man Singh I—commander of Emperor Akbar's imperial army.

Bhangarh quickly evolved into a bustling commercial fortress boasting over 10,000 residents, multi-story merchant Havelis, gemstone bazaars, and five concentric rings of massive fortified stone gates.

ACT III // THE BROKEN OATH & THE ECLIPSE

The Hubris of Ajab Singh & The Falling Shadow

For generations, Madho Singh’s dynasty honored the hermit’s boundary. But when his ambitious descendant, Raja Ajab Singh, ascended the throne, imperial arrogance eclipsed religious reverence.

Ajab Singh ordered grand additional storeys to be built atop the royal palace to dwarf surrounding fortresses. As the winter sun set on the final day of construction, the towering stone ramparts cast a monstrous dark shadow that crept up the mountain slope—swallowing Balu Nath’s sacred sanctuary.

Princess Ratnavati

THE JEWEL OF RAJPUTANA // AGE 18

Daughter of Chatr Singh, Ratnavati’s ethereal beauty and virtue were celebrated throughout northern India. Her grace was said to bring prosperity to Bhangarh, inspiring poets and artisans across kingdoms.

ACT IV // THE BEAUTY & THE OCCULTIST

The Jewel of Rajasthan

As Ratnavati reached her eighteenth year, royal suitors arrived from across Mewar, Marwar, and Delhi bearing lavish gifts of gold, horses, and jewels, competing for an alliance that would unite Rajput royalty.

Yet among those who gazed upon her was a reclusive master of dark sorcery dwelling on the city's outskirts: the Tantrik Singhia Sevda. Aware that an untouchable occultist could never wed a royal princess through honor, Singhia plotted to possess her through dark magic.

ACT V // 1720 AD : THE MIDNIGHT CARNAGE

The Bloodbath of Bhangarh

Singhia’s prophecy manifested with savage speed. In 1720 AD, a bitter feud erupted between Bhangarh and neighboring Ajabgarh, leading to a midnight assault that transformed the golden city into a graveyard.

1720 AD // NIGHT SIEGE

The Breach of Ajabgarh

Armies of Ajab Singh scaled the stone battlements in the dead of night. Street by street, the defenses collapsed. Thousands of sleeping inhabitants were slain in their beds, fulfilling the prophecy of total destruction.

MIDNIGHT // PALACE MASSACRE

The Slaying of the Court

Invaders stormed the upper royal tiers. Princess Ratnavati and the entire royal retinue were executed inside the citadel. No heir survived to claim the throne; blood stained the sandstone courts.

1783 AD // THE CHALISA FAMINE

The Permanent Desolation

The catastrophic Chalisa famine depopulated the entire Aravalli basin. The few remaining survivors fled south. Superstition took root, and for 250 years, no human dared resettle within the cursed ramparts.
